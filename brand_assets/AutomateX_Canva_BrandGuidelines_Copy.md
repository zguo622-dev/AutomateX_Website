## 2. Logo

```
The AutomateX mark is an interlocking 'A' and 'X' monogram in an orange-to-coral gradient. The mark carries our identity across every surface and is the only element of the brand that must never be modified.

Variations:
• Bare mark — favicon, social avatar, icon-only contexts.
• Logo with name (dark) — canonical horizontal lockup on light backgrounds.
• Logo with name (white) — canonical reversed lockup on dark backgrounds.

Clearspace: maintain a minimum clear area equal to the height of the X in the monogram on all four sides.

Minimum sizes: 40 px digital, 15 mm print, 16×16 px favicon (bare mark only).

Logo gradient: #FB7B3A → #F96532 → #ED4A3B.

Never: stretch, recolour the gradient, add shadows, rotate, or place on busy or low-contrast backgrounds. Never apply effects from Canva that change the monogram's geometry or colour.
```

## 4. Typography

```
AutomateX runs on a two-family pairing.

Display and headings: Montserrat. Used for hero headlines, section titles, and any short copy that needs to land. Weights in regular rotation: Bold 700, SemiBold 600, Medium 500. Avoid Montserrat for paragraphs longer than two lines — it loses readability at body size.

Body and reading copy: Open Sans. Used for paragraphs, captions, lists, and any block longer than a heading. Weights in rotation: Regular 400 for body, SemiBold 600 for in-line emphasis, Italic for quotes.

The pairing is fixed. Do not substitute, even when Canva suggests alternatives.
```

Size scale (drop into a Canva Aa preview block, one per heading sample):

```
Display — Montserrat Bold — 36 pt — hero headlines, cover pages
H1 — Montserrat Bold — 28 pt — page titles
H2 — Montserrat SemiBold — 20 pt — section headings
H3 — Montserrat Medium — 16 pt — sub-sections
Body — Open Sans Regular — 14 pt — paragraphs
Caption — Open Sans Regular — 11 pt — captions and footnotes
```

If the Canva template uses 6 or 8 Aa preview blocks, fill each one with one of the lines above.

## 5. Brand voice

```
AutomateX speaks like a senior rail engineer who happens to be fluent in AI. Direct. Rail-fluent. No marketing fluff.

Tone in one line: peer-to-peer, evidence-led, never breathless.

The tagline that runs across all live surfaces: "Engineers engineering."

The promise that opens the conversation: "Where rail domain expertise meets AI. Built by a rail engineer, for rail teams."

The sub-message that converts: "Find your X. Automate the right thing first."
```

House rules (drop as a second paragraph or a bullet list if the template supports lists):

```
House rules.
1. No em dashes. Use full stops, commas, colons, or semicolons. Always.
2. No consultancy cliches. Ban: "in today's competitive landscape", "leverage synergies", "best practices", "drive value", "unlock".
3. No fabricated facts. Speak in general terms rather than inventing numbers, team sizes, or specifics.
4. UK spelling. Judgement not judgment. Optimised not optimized. Customise not customize.
5. The audience is technically credible and time-poor. Get to the point in the first sentence.
6. Avoid "it isn't X, it's Y" constructions. They read as AI-generated.
```

Bill quotes that re-use cleanly in external copy:

```
"We should learn to say no to the things AI can do better."

"Your seniors got into rail to solve problems and exercise engineering judgement, not to feel stuck in admin."
```

## 6. Photography (or "Image style")

```
AutomateX uses photography sparingly and only where it adds proof, not decoration.

Approved photo categories:
• Founder portraits of Bill Guo. Formal headshot for proposals, media-style for press and LinkedIn, portrait for award announcements.
• Live deployment evidence. Station photography, drone survey captures, engineering deliverables under AI-assisted workflow.
• Award credentials. ICE Asset Resilience Merit Award, YRP 2023, Best Project trophies.

What we avoid: stock photography of generic offices, generic AI imagery (glowing brains, blue circuit boards), aspirational lifestyle shots, hands-on-keyboard cliches.

Treatment: photography is presented full-bleed with no decorative crops or filters. Where photos appear on dark surfaces, no border is needed. On light surfaces, leave at least 16 px clear space.
```

## 7. Graphics

```
The AutomateX visual system uses a small, deliberate set of repeatable graphics.

Three-way Venn diagram. The "Domain Knowledge × Engineering Capability × AI-Native" overlap is the visual shorthand for our positioning. Light and dark variants exist. The orange triple intersection always contains the AutomateX mark.

P.A.C.E-D adoption curve. The five-phase exponential curve from "Status Quo" to "Digitalise" is the canonical journey diagram. Use it whenever the long-term transformation arc needs to land.

Knowledge graph render. Subtle particle pattern used as a background texture on the website's research and PACE OS sections. Reusable for slide backgrounds when copy needs to breathe.

QR codes. Generated for site, LinkedIn, and event collateral. Always carried with a 16 px white clear margin.

Never decorate slides with abstract Canva shapes. The Venn, the curve, the knowledge graph, and the brand mark are the visual vocabulary. Stay within it.

## 8. Iconography

```
Icons follow a single specification: 2 px stroke, three variants.
• Filled (orange #F96532) for primary actions and highlighted elements.
• Outlined (Dark Navy #1A1F36 on light, White on dark) for navigation and section labels.
• Ghost (40% opacity) for secondary or muted contexts.

Icons must be geometric, not illustrative. No emoji, no hand-drawn styles, no skeuomorphic shading. When Canva suggests an icon, prefer minimal line-art versions or our brand mark; reject illustrated icon sets.

Sizing: 16, 20, 24, 32, 40 px. Stay on this scale.
```

## 9. Brand application

```
The same palette, type, and voice should produce visibly different surfaces. Use this matrix when starting any new artefact.

Presentations — dark theme on Navy Deep #0D1117. Montserrat titles, Open Sans body. Orange used only for headlines and key data, never as a background fill.

Word documents and proposals — light theme on White. Dark Navy body text. Orange used sparingly on headings and accent lines. Open Sans 11 pt body. Render via the AutomateX-Document-Template.docx and the render-branded-docx.py script.

Email signatures — light theme. Logo at 40 px. Montserrat for name, Open Sans for credentials and contact details. Orange divider rule. Links in Primary Orange.

LinkedIn — dark theme banners and post infographics. Subtle gradient backgrounds. Logo plus tagline prominent. Carbon black background works at the highest contrast.

Business cards — dark Navy on the front with centred logo. White on the back with contact details and an orange accent bar across the top edge.

Website (automatex.uk) — mixed theme. Dark hero and research sections, light content areas in between. Orange CTAs. Consistent type scale across viewports.
```

If your template has a "What we measure" or "Methodology" section, paste this. Otherwise skip.

```
AutomateX measures every rail firm against five operational pillars. These are the same pillars the AI Readiness Assessment scores.

01 Hero Dependency. How resilient delivery is to senior absence and knowledge loss.
02 Expertise Leverage. How much senior time becomes output, and how cleanly capability scales with the pipeline.
03 Continuous Improvement. How much better each project makes the firm.
